# Methodological Limitations & Analytical Guardrails

1. **Descriptive, non-causal design:** None of the five SQL queries, Tableau views, or supplementary panel regressions identify causal policy effects. Co-movement between Germany's renewable expansion, declining generation concentration, rising 2021–2023 electricity prices, and worsening household hardship does not prove that renewable expansion or market concentration caused price or poverty outcomes.
2. **Benchmark sensitivity:** Germany's 2023 electricity-generation concentration (`57.100%`) sits below the all-reporter median (`-6.255 pp`, rank `21/35`) and slightly below the EU-27 median (`-2.340 pp`, rank `15/27`), yet sits well above the Big-6 median (`+14.955 pp`, rank `2/6`). Presenting any single cohort without stating the decision context risks cherry-picking.
3. **Cross-country reporting differences:** Eurostat `nrg_ind_market` relies on voluntary national reporting and uses the same missing marker (`:`) for unavailable and confidential observations. Several economies (Austria, the Netherlands, Turkey, Norway) omit largest-firm shares (`e_cap_top`, `e_gen_top`), and non-EU reporters vary in market size and regulatory history.
4. **Legitimate extreme or negative reported values:**
   - `ren_elec_pct` exceeds `100%` in net hydro-exporting economies (up to `120.01%`) under Eurostat RED II accounting.
   - `elec_price_tax_share_pct` and `gas_price_tax_share_pct` turn negative in several countries during 2022–2023 (e.g., Austria, Greece, Ireland, Luxembourg) where direct government energy subsidies/rebates pushed all-tax prices below ex-tax prices.
   - `utility_arrears_income_gap_pp` can be negative in isolated country-years (`ME 2013`, `MT 2023`) where `above60` arrears exceeded `lowinc` arrears.
5. **Combined ex-tax electricity component:** Eurostat `nrg_pc_204` (`X_TAX`) aggregates wholesale supply costs, grid/network charges, and supplier margins into a single ex-tax price. This repository does **not** decompose the `+85.40%` German ex-tax increase (`€0.1579 → €0.2928/kWh`) into wholesale vs. network vs. margin contributions.
6. **Market structure does not directly prove market conduct:** Neither generation concentration (`CMPY_EG5`) nor gas-retailer counts/density (`GRTL`, `retailers_per_twh`) measures firm pricing power, switching friction, regional *Stadtwerke* overlap, or retail markups.
7. **Stored income-gap definition vs. total-population gap:** The stored columns `unable_warm_income_gap_pp` and `utility_arrears_income_gap_pp` represent `B_60 (low-income) − A_60 (above-60% median income)`, **not** `low-income − total population`. Query 5 therefore computes direct `low-income − total` gaps (`4.5 → 6.1 pp` thermal; `2.8 → 3.9 pp` arrears) from the component columns to match the policy question.
8. **Germany 2020 EU-SILC break & static 2013–2023 scope:** Germany recorded a documented EU-SILC methodology break in 2020 (`unable_warm_total_pct` spiked temporarily to `7.0%` before returning to `3.3%` in 2021), which is why social-resilience comparisons anchor on `2021 → 2023`. The dataset is a static `2013–2023` snapshot (not real-time or automatically refreshed).
